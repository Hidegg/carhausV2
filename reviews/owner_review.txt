CARHAUS V2 — OWNER REVIEW
Written from the perspective of a 10+ year carwash operator
Date: March 2026
==========================================================================

I've been running carwashes for over a decade. I've used spreadsheets, I've
used generic POS systems that were built for restaurants and shoehorned into
a carwash context, I've used paper logs, and I've even had a custom Access
database built by a guy who disappeared six months later. I know exactly what
a carwash operation needs because I've lived the frustration of not having it.

So here's my honest take on Carhaus V2.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE CORE LOOP — WHAT ACTUALLY MATTERS EVERY DAY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

The daily workflow is right. You come in, you mark who's present, a car rolls
up, you punch in the plate, pick the service, pick the washer, take the money,
done. The fact that the form remembers returning customers by plate and
auto-fills their car type and brand — that's the one thing that saves the most
time at a busy location. A regular showing up at 8am on a Tuesday doesn't want
to wait while someone types their details again. That autocomplete is not a nice
to have, it's essential, and it's here.

The 4am day boundary is something most software developers would never think of
but every carwash operator will immediately understand. We don't stop at
midnight. A crew finishing a late detail job at 1am shouldn't close out the
previous day's numbers — they're still on the same shift. Someone who actually
understands the business built that feature.

The multi-location support is solid. If you're running two or three sites
(which is the only way this business scales), you need one place to see
everything. The admin overview with totals per location side by side is
something I'd genuinely use every morning before I even get in the car.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
REVENUE AND PAYMENT HANDLING — MOSTLY GOOD, ONE FRUSTRATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

CASH, CARD, CONTRACT, PROTOCOL — those four cover about 95% of a Romanian
carwash's reality. The CONTRACT and PROTOCOL distinction matters more than it
might seem: fleet contracts (companies that bring their cars regularly at a
negotiated rate) are different from protocol accounts (partner arrangements
with hotels, dealerships, etc.) and keeping them separate in your revenue
breakdown helps you understand which B2B relationships are actually pulling
weight.

CURS (pending/deferred payment) is the right call for that grey zone where a
regular customer says "put it on my tab" or a company sends their invoice
weekly. The fact that it's tracked separately and there's a dedicated screen
for it is good. What's frustrating is there's no alert or escalation when a
CURS invoice ages past a certain point. Right now you have to remember to look
at that screen. Unpaid invoices should be chasing you, not the other way
around. After a certain number of days — say 30 — I want a flag, ideally a
message to my phone.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
WORKERS AND COMMISSIONS — THE RIGHT FOUNDATION, MISSING THE SECOND FLOOR
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

Tracking who washed which car and what commission they earned per service is
the foundation of every performance conversation I've ever had with a washer.
"Here's what you did this week, here's what you earned" — that's how you
motivate good workers and identify the ones coasting. The per-service
commission structure (different rates for AUTOTURISM, SUV, VAN) is exactly
how real carwash commission agreements work.

The presence toggle is fine as a daily "who showed up" marker, but it's one
small step from scheduling. What I actually need is to know in advance who's
coming in on Saturday, because Saturday is my busiest day and if I have three
people rostered and only two show up, that's a problem I want to see before
it happens, not after. There's no scheduling here. That's a real gap.

Bigger issue: there's no way to print or export a worker's monthly earnings
summary. At the end of the month when I'm calculating what I owe everyone,
I need a document. Right now I'd have to screenshot the screen or do the math
myself. This needs a simple export — even a PDF with the worker's name, the
period, the services done, and the total commission. It doesn't have to be
fancy. It just has to exist.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
REPORTING — STRONG WHERE IT MATTERS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

The historical drill-down (annual → monthly → weekly) is genuinely useful.
Most carwash software gives you a date range filter and calls it "reporting."
This actually lets you understand your own patterns. You can look at a slow
month and drill into which week was the problem and whether it was weather,
staffing, or just a bad stretch.

The service breakdown by type is the thing I'd actually use to make pricing
decisions. If SPALARE TAPITERIE (upholstery) makes up 3% of services but 15%
of revenue, I want to know that and I want to push it. That data is in here.

What's missing is any way to compare location performance directly over a
shared time period. Right now I can see each location's numbers and mentally
do the comparison. I want to see "Straulesti vs. Caranfil, last 90 days" in
one chart. Two locations running the same brand should be benchmarkable
against each other.

No export to Excel or CSV anywhere. That's a hard stop for any owner who
also has an accountant. My accountant doesn't log into apps — she works with
files. A simple CSV export on any report table would solve this immediately.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
CLIENT DATABASE — GDPR IS THERE, BUT THE DATABASE IS UNDERUSED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

The client database captures plate, brand, type, phone, email, and consent
fields. The consent tracking (GDPR, newsletter) is the legally correct thing
to do in Romania, and I'm glad it's in there — one audit notice because
you can't demonstrate consent is expensive. That box is checked.

But the database is barely being used as a business tool. I have a client who
has been in 47 times and spent over 3,000 RON with me, and the app has no
awareness of that relationship. No loyalty tracking. No way to flag VIP clients.
No way to send a message to clients who opted in to the newsletter. The data
is collected and then sits there. That's wasted potential.

The client detail page (full service history per plate) is useful for disputes
— "when did you last bring this car in, what was done" — but there's no
segmentation, no revenue tier, no visit frequency bucket. Even basic grouping
(frequent / occasional / lapsed) would make the database actionable.

No ability to add a note to a client. Sometimes a customer has a specific
preference ("always use the soft cloth on my hood"), or there's a billing
arrangement I need to remember, or they're a difficult customer I want to flag.
A free-text note field per client would cost almost nothing to build and I'd
use it constantly.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE SETTINGS AND PRICING MODULE — WORKS, BUT RIGID
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

Prices are organized by service type and vehicle class (AUTOTURISM, SUV, VAN).
That's the right structure. Location-specific price overrides are also right —
my two locations are in different neighborhoods and the market rate is different
at each one. That's handled.

What's not handled: time-based pricing. If I want to run a "Monday discount"
or a "weekday morning special," there's no mechanism for it. I'd have to
manually change the prices on Monday morning and change them back Tuesday.
That's how mistakes get made. Even a simple "this price applies on these days"
would be a meaningful upgrade.

Also: there's no way to see a price history. If a price was changed three
months ago and I want to know what it was before, there's no record. For a
business that does contract pricing with companies, that audit trail matters.

The "ALTE SERVICII" (other services) catch-all with a zero price is clever as
an escape hatch, but it means anything unusual ends up as undescribed revenue
with no breakdown. You lose data quality every time someone uses that bucket.
Even requiring a short description when ALTE SERVICII is selected would help.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT IS COMPLETELY MISSING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. INVOICING
   This is the biggest gap by a significant margin. Every B2B customer (fleet
   contracts, protocol accounts, corporate clients) needs an invoice. Right
   now there's nothing. No invoice template, no invoice number series, no PDF
   generation, no VAT calculation. I cannot legally request payment from a
   company without an invoice. This needs to exist before this software can
   be the only tool on a B2B-heavy location.

2. END-OF-DAY CASH RECONCILIATION
   When a manager closes out at the end of the day, there should be a
   reconciliation step: "the system says you collected X RON in cash today,
   count the drawer and confirm." Right now the system records payments but
   doesn't close a shift. There's no daily close, no float management, no
   variance tracking. Every carwash I've run has had a cash discrepancy at
   some point. You need a paper trail.

3. SHIFT CLOSE / MANAGER SIGN-OFF
   Related to the above: a manager should be able to "close the day" with a
   timestamp and their name attached. This creates accountability. Right now
   there's no concept of a closed day — theoretically someone could add a
   service from three weeks ago. There should be a lock once a day is signed
   off.

4. NOTIFICATIONS
   No SMS, no push notification, no email alert of any kind. I want to know
   when daily revenue hits a threshold (good day — celebrate it). I want to
   know if a manager hasn't logged a single service by 10am on a Saturday
   (something is wrong). I want my CURS invoices to remind me they're aging.
   Notifications are how a management app becomes a management tool instead
   of just a ledger.

5. WORKER PAYOUT EXPORT
   As mentioned above: a printable monthly earnings summary per worker. This
   is not optional if you're running payroll.

6. OFFLINE MODE
   Carwash locations have had internet outages. If the internet is down and
   the manager can't pull up the app, do they go back to paper? The business
   doesn't stop because the router's having a bad day. Some level of offline
   capability (even just the form, with sync when connection returns) would
   make this tool viable as the single system of record.

7. MINIMUM VIABLE LOYALTY
   Nothing complex. Just a counter. "This client has visited X times. After
   visit 10, offer them a free interior service." Even a simple punch-card
   style counter that managers can see on the plate lookup would keep customers
   coming back and justify collecting all this client data.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT WORKS REALLY WELL (AND SHOULD NEVER BE BROKEN)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

— The plate autocomplete with car info pre-fill. Fast, accurate, essential.
— The 4am day boundary. Whoever built this knows carwash operations.
— The CURS payment type and its dedicated tracking screen.
— Per-service, per-vehicle-class commission structure.
— Location-specific price overrides.
— The annual → monthly → weekly historical drill-down.
— The GDPR consent fields built into the service form (not bolted on).
— The role separation (manager sees only their location, admin sees all).
— The admin overview with all locations in one view.
— The multi-service entry per car on the same form submission.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
OVERALL VERDICT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

Carhaus V2 is a real carwash tool, not a generic business app with a
carwash skin on it. The daily workflow is right. The financial categories
are right. The worker commission model is right. The architecture (multi-
location, role-based) is right. Whoever built this either ran a carwash or
spent serious time in one.

For a single-location, cash-heavy, B2C operation, this is close to complete.
You could run a location on this today and cover 80% of what you need.

For a multi-location, B2B-active operation (contract clients, fleet accounts,
corporate protocols), the invoicing gap is a blocker. You can't run that
business without invoices. Everything else is a quality-of-life improvement;
invoicing is a legal and financial requirement.

Priority order for what to build next, from a pure operator standpoint:

  1. Invoice generation (PDF, with VAT, with invoice series) — BLOCKER for B2B
  2. End-of-day cash reconciliation and shift close — accountability gap
  3. Worker monthly earnings export (PDF/CSV) — payroll requirement
  4. CSV/Excel export on all report tables — accountant requirement
  5. Aging alerts for CURS invoices — money recovery
  6. Client notes field — daily operational need
  7. Basic loyalty counter — retention
  8. Shift scheduling — staffing visibility
  9. Time-based pricing — promotional flexibility
  10. Location comparison charts — multi-site management

If I were evaluating this as a software purchase for my operation, I'd buy it
today for the B2C side and wait for invoicing before fully migrating my
contract clients off whatever I'm currently using. That's not a dismissal —
that's a genuine compliment. Most software in this space doesn't even get the
daily workflow right.
